Play 1: Strategy at the Speed of the Market

Capabilities: Multi-model · Subagents · Skills · Google Search · ~25 min

Why a CxO cares: A strategy refresh that normally takes analysts three to six weeks and a consultancy invoice now takes fifteen minutes — grounded in live public filings, challenged by a second frontier model, and rendered in your company's verified brand guidelines.

Analysing

Play 1 needs a publicly listed company — yours, a peer, or a competitor.

Stage 1: Strategic Assessment with Live Web Grounding (~8 min incl. approval)

  1. Open the Gemini Agents Desktop App and click New Task in the left sidebar.
  2. Confirm the model picker is set to Auto or Gemini 3.8 Flash and Approvals are set to Approve for me (Auto-review).
  3. Copy and paste the prompt below into the chat box and press ENTER.
terminal Step A Prompt · Gemini (Auto / Gemini 3.8 Flash) ⏱ ~1 min, then approve
/strategic-assessment-skill Act as a Senior Strategy Consultant. Conduct a strategic assessment of [Your Company] based on its most recent earnings report and financial performance. Include: 1. Core Value Proposition & Monetization (primary revenue streams, fastest-growing segments, headwinds). 2. Strategic Initiatives & Market Positioning (key strategic bets, AI/Cloud investments). 3. SWOT Analysis (Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats). Keep it structured and concise. Use 3 parallel sub-agents (earnings, peers, regulatory) for the research.
check_circle Expected Outcome

The research plan appears as progress steps at the top and the agent waits. After Approved, go ahead. three sub-agents (earnings, peers, regulatory) run in the Subagents panel, then <company>_strategic_assessment.html opens from the file carousel: KPI strip, segment chart, strategic bets, SWOT 2×2, dated sources. The memo is a Google Doc; the chat reply is a ~150-word BLUF.

Approve the plan: the research plan appears as progress steps at the top of the task and the agent waits for you — type Approved, go ahead. Three sub-agents (earnings, peers, regulatory) then start in parallel; open the Subagents panel to watch them.

What to look for: Click the generated Google Drive / local HTML link to open <company>_strategic_assessment.html — featuring an executive KPI strip, segment revenue breakdown, strategic bets, interactive SWOT 2×2, and footnoted SEC/annual report citations.

Stage 2: Red Team from a Different Model Family (~5 min)

  1. Stay in the exact same task. Click the Model picker at the bottom of the chat box and switch to Claude Opus.
  2. Copy and paste the prompt below and press ENTER.
terminal Step B Prompt · Switch Picker to Claude Opus (Same Task) ⏱ ~5 min
/strategy-red-team-skill Switch hats: challenge the assessment above as an activist investor, a rival CEO and a regulator. Where is it wrong, what did it miss, and what should we do differently?
check_circle Expected Outcome

A red-team review from an activist investor, a rival CEO and a regulator, usually “holds with amendments”, with ranked fixes, in red_team_review.html and a Google Doc.

Key takeaway: No single-model lock-in. You get a genuine second opinion from a different frontier model family — with the full conversation context and governance preserved.

  2. The Gemini Agents Desktop App will ground Google's official brand palette, typography, and logo online, generate bespoke editorial visuals and code-driven charts, and upload the finished PowerPoint deck directly to Google Slides alongside an interactive HTML presentation and Word executive memo.

A branded 6-slide investor deck: the HTML deck in the carousel, the Google Slides link and an executive memo as a Google Doc.

Here, your AI Chief of Staff reads across Gmail, Calendar, and Drive, separates signal from noise, remembers your standing rules once, and runs automatically at 08:00 every morning.

Important for Approval Cards in Plays 2 & 3: If the Gemini Agents Desktop App asks permission to create a Gmail draft or calendar hold, click Allow once — never Always allow. Play 4 relies on Gmail actions still requiring verification.

Capabilities: Guardian Auto-Review · Approvals · Audit Trail · Admin Controls · ~11 min

Scenario: Every board asks the same question: if agents can read our email and act for us, what stops them doing the wrong thing? Next, we deliberately ask the agent to email the financials to a personal address. The Gemini Agents Desktop App's independent reviewer (Guardian) and policy guardrails intercept both attempts and produce an audit-ready evidence pack for your CISO, General Counsel, and CRO.

Restore the Reporting System

Folder: agy_workspace/ · Commands: /browser · /plan · /goal · /learn · /schedule

The reporting system is down

Cymbal runs a digital commerce platform — 12 services behind cymbal.shop and the Cymbal apps. The Platform Reliability Control Tower (./cymbal-control-tower) is the reporting system leadership runs the business on: availability, error budgets, incidents and deploys for every service, and the numbers the CTO takes into Monday’s 09:00 ops review. On Friday afternoon the platform tools team shipped v1.4.0. Since then the Control Tower is effectively down: it takes seconds to load, its headline numbers are garbage, the latency chart is empty, the new team view shows nothing — and its health check still reports operational.

Your role: the engineering leader who restores the reporting system before Monday — without writing a line of code yourself. You decide; the agents do the work.

1

See the outage, and find out why (/browser)

Start with what leadership sees, then let the agent find the cause. Look at the screenshot and diagnosis it returns — and open http://localhost:3000 in your own browser to feel how slow it is.

terminal Prompt 1 · Browser Diagnosis
/browser Start the Control Tower (cymbal-control-tower) on port 3000 and show me a screenshot of what leadership sees. Then diagnose it: compare Friday's v1.4.0 release with v1.3.0 in the git history and tell me what broke, why, and how serious each problem is.
check_circle Expected Outcome

A red banner (overview API took ~3 s, budget 300 ms), a run-on Requests per minute value, NaN% error rate and error budgets, a flat latency chart reading “p95 1 ms”, availability falsely Critical at 99.86%, and an empty team filter — each traced to the v1.4.0 diff: per-bucket queries and a blocking cluster call, a formatted string where a number was expected, a renamed field, a unit change to seconds, and a slug-vs-name mismatch.

2

Get a plan before any code changes (/plan)

Governance starts before code is written. The prompt fixes forward and keeps the team filter the VP of Engineering asked for — if you would rather roll back to v1.3.0, say so when you review the plan. Then do not click Approve or Proceed — the next prompt approves it.

terminal Prompt 2 · Implementation Plan
/plan Write an implementation plan to fix every problem you found in cymbal-control-tower while keeping v1.4.0's team filter: what changes in the backend and in the frontend, the API contract both sides will follow, and how you will prove it works (tests plus a real-browser check). Stop after writing the plan — don't modify any code.
check_circle Expected Outcome

An implementation_plan.md artifact with backend and frontend changes, an explicit API contract (raw numbers, milliseconds, v1.3.0 field names, a working team filter) and a test-plus-browser verification plan — and no code touched yet.

3

Approve and delegate to a parallel team (/goal)

Act as the engineering director: approve the plan by sending /goal, which splits the work between a backend and a frontend subagent working to one shared contract, and keeps going until every check passes (why fan-out works).

terminal Prompt 3 · Parallel Subagents & Goal Mode
/goal Approve the plan and execute it. Spawn 2 subagents in parallel — one owns the backend (cymbal-control-tower/app.py), one owns the frontend (cymbal-control-tower/static/) — both working to the agreed API contract. Then run the smoke tests plus a new test for every defect, and loop until everything passes and /api/overview answers in under 300 ms.
check_circle Expected Outcome

Two subagents run in parallel — backend: batched queries, raw numbers, milliseconds, team filter; frontend: formatting, error-budget field, chart units — then the smoke tests plus one test per defect pass and /api/overview answers in milliseconds.

4

Check it like a user would (/browser)

Terminal output is not proof. Ask for a fresh screenshot and a line-by-line read of the dashboard. In your own browser, hard-refresh the page (Cmd+Shift+R on Mac, Ctrl+Shift+R on Windows) to see the fix.

terminal Prompt 4 · Browser Check
/browser Restart the Control Tower on port 3000, reload the dashboard without the browser cache, and show me a screenshot. Check the banner, every KPI, the latency chart and the team filter, and tell me anything that still looks wrong.
5

Turn the lesson into a rule, then test it (/learn)

Workspace rules (.agents/rules/cymbal-governance.md) are institutional memory that every future agent and subagent follows. First look at the rules you already have:

terminal Prompt 5a · Inspect the Rules
Show me the workspace governance rules in .agents/rules/cymbal-governance.md and summarise in two sentences how they would have stopped this release. 
# .agents/rules/cymbal-governance.md
1. Performance budget: Control Tower API endpoints must answer within 300 ms (p95). No blocking calls or sleeps
   inside request handlers, and no per-row query loops — fetch in batches.
2. API contracts: backend and frontend changes ship together. Field names and units never change silently.
3. Production changes: agents may investigate and scale services on their own; a production rollback needs an
   approval recorded by the on-call engineer.
4. Agent decisions: agents follow company-twin/decision_rights.yaml; only approvals in the Decision Inbox count.

The rules existed — but nothing checked them before v1.4.0 shipped. Add a release gate so the governance is enforced automatically the next time anyone, person or agent, changes the Control Tower:

terminal Prompt 5b · Release Gate
/learn Add a release-gate rule to .agents/rules/cymbal-governance.md: no change to cymbal-control-tower is complete until its tests pass, /api/overview answers in under 300 ms, and the dashboard has been checked in a real browser with a screenshot.

Prove the rule works — ask for something that breaks it and watch the agent push back:

terminal Prompt 5c · Prove It
Quick tweak for tomorrow's demo: add a 1-second sleep to /api/overview so people can see the loading state, then mark it done.
check_circle Expected Outcome

A fifth rule (the release gate) in cymbal-governance.md; the 1-second-sleep request is challenged with a reference to the performance budget and the release gate.
